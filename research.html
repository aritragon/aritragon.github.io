Environment-dependent pairwise velocities

The spread of velocities around the mean pairwise velocity is usually treated as noise and marginalised over. We showed that much of this dispersion is signal: galaxies in dense and underdense environments stream in opposite senses, and these motions cancel when everything is averaged together. Splitting halos or galaxies by local density and measuring the streaming velocity in each subsample removes the cancellation and boosts the total signal-to-noise several-fold.

I am now developing this density-split framework further to extract more cosmological information from existing galaxy survey data, with applications to kSZ and redshift-space distortion analyses.

Pairwise kSZ estimators and tests of dark energy

The pairwise kSZ effect measures how pairs of galaxy clusters fall towards each other, a direct tracer of the growth of structure. I developed cross-pairwise estimators that pair clusters with galaxies from a spectroscopic survey instead of with other clusters, which greatly increases the number of usable pairs.

Fisher forecasts for Simons Observatory and CMB-S4 combined with DESI show that the cross-pairwise approach tightens constraints on the dark energy equation of state (w0, wa) by factors of about 2–3 and on the growth index γ by factors of about 4–6 compared with the standard pairwise estimator. The same idea makes the faint polarised pairwise kSZ signal detectable with CMB-S4 when cross-correlated with a galaxy survey of a few billion objects.

Polarised kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect

When CMB photons scatter off free electrons that are moving across our line of sight, the transverse motion generates a small polarisation signal at second order in the velocity. I showed that this polarised kSZ signal separates into a blackbody part and a y-type spectral distortion. Because of its distinct frequency dependence, the y-type part can be pulled apart from primary and lensing B modes and from the thermal SZ effect, giving a new probe of the reionisation history and of cosmology that is not limited by the same cosmic variance as the primary CMB.

We also developed a novel pairwise estimator for the polarised kinetic SZ effect arising from the transverse peculiar velocity of galaxy clusters. We find that cross-pairing clusters with galaxies from a large overlapping galaxy survey having a few billion galaxies will enable us to detect the pairwise pkSZ effect with CMB-HD.

Earlier work: integrable many-body systems

Before moving to cosmology I studied a one-dimensional hyperbolic Calogero model that remains classically integrable even inside a strong, box-like confining potential. Using a duality between the particle and field descriptions, we constructed multi-soliton solutions and studied their collisions and quench dynamics, with possible relevance to trapped cold atomic gases.
